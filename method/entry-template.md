# Library Entry Template

Every entry answers the same four questions, in the same order, so that a reader can use any of them without re-learning the format. Target length: 450–600 words. One page.

---

## `ID · Topic name`

**Framework home** — TSC number and title, proficiency level, and the exact K/A items this topic serves. This is the anchor: if a topic cannot be tied to a K or an A, it does not belong in a TSC-mapped course.

**What it actually is** — 120–200 words. Mechanically accurate, no metaphors yet. The reader should be able to explain it to a colleague after one read.

**Why it matters at work** — 2–3 sentences. The cost of not knowing it: wasted time, bad output, a compliance breach, a wrong decision.

**The student lens** — How a learner should hold this idea:
- the mental model to install (one image or sentence)
- two or three misconceptions that reliably show up in a classroom, stated as the learner would say them
- one practice task they can do in five minutes with a live tool

**The adult educator lens** — How to teach it:
- **Analogy**: one, concrete, from Singapore daily life or ordinary office work. One strong analogy beats five weak ones.
- **Three-minute demo**: exactly what to type on screen, in order, including the failure case.
- **Board sketch**: what you draw, described in words so anyone can reproduce it.
- **Where it goes wrong in class**: the question that will derail the room, and the honest answer.
- **Check for understanding**: one question whose answer proves they got it (not "any questions?").

**Say this out loud** — one sentence you can use verbatim in class. Short, plain, memorable.

**Content hook** — a LinkedIn opener (first two lines, the part that shows before "see more") and one carousel or reel idea.

---

### Writing rules

1. **No invented statistics, studies or dates.** If a number is not certain, describe the direction of the effect instead.
2. **Name the level.** Basic, Intermediate, Advanced. A trainer who blurs levels is a trainer whose course gets mis-mapped.
3. **The analogy must survive contact with a sceptic.** Test it: does it break if you push it one step further? If yes, say where it breaks.
4. **Include one failure.** Every concept has a "this is where it bites" story. That is the part learners remember.
5. **Singapore footing.** Use examples from local work: a hawker menu, a tender document, a CPF statement, a Singlish email, a school report book.
6. **Never punch down at learners.** The tone is "this is genuinely confusing, and here is the handle", not "most people get this wrong".
