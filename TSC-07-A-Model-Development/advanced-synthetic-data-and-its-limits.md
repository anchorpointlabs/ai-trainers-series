# Synthetic data: the substitute teacher with a blind spot

> **TSC #7 Generative AI Model Development and Fine Tuning · Advanced · Knowledge K7**


> **Framework text (verbatim):** "Advanced synthetic data generation and limitations."
>
> **TSC #7** Generative AI Model Development and Fine Tuning · **Advanced** · Knowledge · Bullet 7 of 20

**One-line version:** Synthetic data buys coverage you cannot collect and inherits every blind spot of the model that made it — so you must know which of the two you are buying.

## What it is

Advanced synthetic data generation is the production of training examples by a model, a simulator or a program, at a scale and specificity that real collection cannot reach: rephrased instructions, generated dialogues, simulated sensor streams, tabular records with privacy guarantees, code and mathematics problems with verified answers. Its limitations are structural, not incidental: a generator can only produce what its distribution covers, and recursive training on generated data erodes the tails.

## Deep expansion

### The concept

Synthetic data works best where you can **verify** the output and where you need **breadth** more than depth. Generate ten thousand SQL questions with known answers and you have real training signal. Generate ten thousand customer complaints and you have plausible text that reflects the generating model's idea of a complaint — including the complaints it never thought of.

That distinction produces the useful rule: *synthetic data is a coverage tool, not a truth tool*. It extends the range of situations a model has seen; it cannot add information about the world that the generator did not already contain. Every serious pipeline therefore pairs generation with filtering — a verifier, a quality classifier or a human sample — because unfiltered generation amplifies the generator's errors rather than averaging them out.

### The sub-concepts

**Distillation-style generation.** A stronger model produces examples that a smaller model learns from, often with rationales. This is the most common industrial use and the one with the most contractual care needed: provider terms, provenance and the right to use outputs for training vary, and a model trained on a competitor's outputs is a commercial question as much as a technical one.

**Programmatic and simulation-based generation.** Physics engines with domain randomisation, renderers, and code-execution-verified problem sets. These have an oracle, so quality is measurable and the tail can be extended deliberately.

**Privacy-motivated synthesis.** Tabular generators trained with differential privacy produce records that preserve statistics without reproducing individuals. The trade-off is fidelity against privacy budget; low budgets produce data too smooth to train on.

**Recursive training and diversity collapse.** Train on your own output, repeat, and the distribution narrows: rare cases disappear first, then the model's output becomes bland and self-similar. The published concern is real even where the extreme version is contested, and the mitigation — mixing real data, keeping a fixed reference corpus, filtering aggressively — is standard.

**Contamination of your own evaluation.** If test items are generated by the same model you are evaluating, the score measures self-consistency. Keep a human-authored, frozen holdout.

### What is current as of November 2026

Synthetic data has become standard practice in post-training pipelines, particularly for instruction-following, tool use and code, where verification is cheap. Current frontier models are widely understood to use substantial generated data alongside human data, with heavy filtering. The open question the field has not closed is long-horizon diversity: generated agent trajectories tend to repeat the same successful paths, which trains confident but narrow agents.

### Common misconceptions

- *"Synthetic data is fake, so it is worthless."* It is invaluable for coverage and format; it is worthless as a source of new facts.
- *"More generated data always helps."* Past a point it flattens the distribution and teaches the model its own habits.
- *"If it passes my generated test set, the model is good."* A generated test set tests agreement with the generator.
- *"Privacy synthesis is automatically safe."* Only with a formal guarantee and a tested budget; naive generators leak.

## How a student should understand it

**Mental model:** a substitute teacher who has read the textbook and never met your class. Excellent for covering the syllabus; useless for knowing which child is struggling.

**Three things they can do after this bullet:**
1. Decide whether a task is a coverage problem or a truth problem, and whether synthesis applies.
2. Design a generation-plus-filter pipeline with a verifier.
3. Keep a human-authored holdout that synthetic data can never touch.

**Misconceptions to kill:** that synthetic equals free, that volume equals diversity, and that a generated benchmark is a benchmark.

## How a trainer explains it

### Analogies that work in a Singapore classroom

**The photocopier that gets worse each generation.** Photocopy a photocopy and the fine print disappears first; the bold headline survives longest. Model collapse erodes exactly the way: rare cases, then nuance, then everything but the stereotype. Where it breaks: a photocopier has no memory of the original, whereas a well-run pipeline keeps the real corpus in the mix.

**The zi-char stall's mise en place.** You can pre-cut vegetables for the dinner rush — that is synthesis as preparation. You cannot pre-cook the fish and call it fresh.

### Worked example with a real tool

Make learners produce a filtered batch, not a batch:

```
We need 5,000 training examples for a tool-calling assistant
that books meeting rooms. We have 200 real hand-labelled ones.

Design the synthetic pipeline:
1. The generation prompt, including the schema and three failure
   cases the generator must also produce (wrong room, past date,
   double booking).
2. The verifier that rejects bad examples automatically, and why
   it is independent of the generator.
3. The mix ratio with the 200 real examples, and what happens to
   the real ones in the mix.
4. How you would detect diversity collapse after two rounds.
```

What to point at afterwards: the strongest answers require the generator to produce *failures* as well as successes, because a dataset of only correct calls teaches a model that every request is valid. The weakest answers generate 5,000 happy paths and call it done.

### Lesson plan (Gagné's nine events)

1. **Gain attention** — show a model that answers 95% of generated tests and 60% of real ones.
2. **State the objective** — design a generate-and-verify pipeline with a frozen holdout.
3. **Recall prior learning** — the manifold hypothesis from `TSC7-Advanced-K1`.
4. **Present the content** — coverage versus truth, verification, collapse, contamination.
5. **Guide** — work the room-booking pipeline together.
6. **Elicit performance** — groups design a pipeline for their own domain.
7. **Give feedback** — critique verifier independence and mix ratios.
8. **Transfer** — each learner names a task where synthesis is justified and one where it is not.

## What the framework doesn't mention but trainers need to know

- **Read the provider terms before generating training data.** Rights to use outputs for training differ, and this is a legal question, not a technical one.
- **Provenance tracking is mandatory.** You will be asked, later, which examples came from where.
- **Human data is a fixed asset.** Protect it, version it, and never let it be overwritten by generated volume.
- **Synthesis is cheapest where the oracle is free** — code, maths, structured formats, simulations.

## Watch out

- **Do not present a synthetic dataset as a solution to a data problem.** It solves coverage, sometimes; it never solves missing truth.
- **Do not skip the holdout discussion.** Learners will otherwise build their own illusion of progress.
- **Do not demo generation without the filter.** The filter is the technique; generation is the commodity.
- **The stale claim to retire:** "we are running out of data, so synthetic data will replace it." In 2026 synthetic data extends real data; it does not substitute for it.
