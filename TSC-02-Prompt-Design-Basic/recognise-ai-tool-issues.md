# Recognise AI tool issues

> **TSC #2 Prompt Design · Basic · Ability A2**

**Bullet (verbatim):** Recognise issues encountered with AI tools.

**What it means:** An AI tool can give a plausible answer that includes a claim not supported by the information provided, or it can use a different layout from the one requested. It may instead refuse a request or display an error message. Recognising an issue means saying what you can see without guessing its cause; an unsupported claim is not necessarily false, and a refusal is not necessarily a fault.

**The learner must be able to:**
- Compare an answer with the supplied note and flag an unsupported detail.
- Check an answer against the requested layout and describe a mismatch.
- Distinguish a refusal and a service error message from a content or layout issue.
- Quote the visible evidence without claiming a cause that has not been established.

**How to teach it in 20 minutes:**
1. Ask: “If an AI answer looks tidy, how can we tell whether it meets the request?” Take two suggestions. (3 min)
2. Use the fictional note in the exercise to explain four checks: compare claims with the note; compare layout with the requested lines; look for a refusal, meaning the tool declines the request; and look for a service error message. Explain that a detail missing from the note is unsupported by that note, not automatically false, and that a refusal is not automatically a fault. A message alone does not establish the cause of an error. (7 min)
3. In pairs, learners run the prompt below. They check the actual notice against the note and line requirements, then classify each invented card and quote the words that support their choice. (8 min)
4. Compare the pairs’ findings with the check below. Ask what they can observe and what they cannot conclude about the cause; distinguish the made-up cards from the actual response. (2 min)

**Exercise (with the exact prompt):**
Setup: Use any chat AI; no files are needed. Work in pairs.

```text
This is a fictional classroom task. Note: "The Green Team meets on Tuesday at 10:00 in Cedar Room."

First write a notice using only the note. The notice must be exactly two lines: line 1 begins "When:" and gives the day and time; line 2 begins "Where:" and gives the place. Do not add facts or a heading above the notice.

Then copy the following four labelled practice cards exactly after the notice, preserving their wording and line breaks. They are invented examples for the same task, not reports of real incidents. Card D is a pretend message from the service. Do not analyse or name the problems.

A:
When: Tuesday at 10:00
Where: Cedar Room; chair: Mei
B:
The Green Team meets on Tuesday at 10:00 in Cedar Room.
C:
I cannot help with that request.
D:
Service temporarily unavailable.
```

Expected result: A two-line notice giving Tuesday at 10:00 and Cedar Room, followed by the four invented cards. Actual outputs can vary.
What to check: Check the actual notice against the note and two-line layout; for cards A–D respectively, flag the unsupported chair, wrong layout, refusal and service error message. Neither C nor D alone establishes a cause.
