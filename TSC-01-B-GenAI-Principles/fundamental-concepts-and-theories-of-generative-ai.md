# Fundamental concepts and theories of generative AI

> **TSC #1 Generative AI Principles and Applications · Basic · Knowledge K2**

**IMDA requirement:** Fundamental concepts of and theories governing generative AI.

**What it means:** GenAI rests on three ideas, none of which you need maths for: **learning from examples** (the model absorbs patterns from training data, it is not programmed with rules), **probability** (it predicts the most likely next word, not a looked-up fact), and **neural networks** (layers of weighted connections that encode those patterns). The mental model to keep: the model read millions of books and now guesses what comes next in that style.

**The learner must be able to:**
- Explain 'learning from examples' vs 'programmed with rules' in one line.
- Describe why the model's output is a prediction, not a retrieval.
- Point to one visible consequence of the probabilistic nature (output varies on re-run).

**Lesson Plan Ideas:**
1. Ask: does ChatGPT 'know' things the way a dictionary does? Collect guesses.
2. Teach the three ideas with the 'student who read every book' picture.
3. Run the same prompt twice and show the output differs — evidence it is prediction, not lookup.
4. Debrief: what pattern, probability and networks mean in their own words.

**Exercise (with the exact prompt):**
```text
Run this twice in a new chat and compare:
"Give me three titles for a talk about saving water."
```
Expected: the two runs differ slightly — proof the model predicts, it does not retrieve a fixed answer.

**Common mistakes:**
- Thinking the model looks answers up in a database.
- Thinking it is programmed with if-then rules by engineers.
- Overestimating understanding: fluency is pattern-matching, not comprehension.
